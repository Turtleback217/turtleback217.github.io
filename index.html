I lead localization across 40 countries. My work spans the systems behind international expansion, the behavior of a live AI feature, and helping teams build with AI.

01 / International expansion

Designing the system behind
twenty new markets.

I designed and built the localization systems for BlaBlaCar’s expansion into twenty new countries. The challenge reached beyond translating a catalogue: the product kept changing, markets needed different approaches, and quality had to remain manageable in languages I don’t speak.

Design the inputs

I researched the markets, recruited and briefed linguists, and turned their expertise into terminology, instructions and examples the systems could use.

Choose the right route

Some markets needed new translations; others could inherit and adapt existing language. Where regional grammar and voice needed more, I built a dedicated process instead of forcing every country through the same workflow.

Keep the product current

I connected the initial catalogue work to continuous updates, with separate translation and quality checks, recovery from interruptions, and delivery back into the product’s content system.

Give every review more than one use

I designed review around real app screens and the content that mattered most. A finding could then improve the reviewed text, matching problems elsewhere, and the instructions used for future translations.

  1. 01Correct the reviewed content
  2. 02Apply the finding elsewhere
  3. 03Improve future guidance

Beyond the launch, these language practices have been recommended internally as a reference for other teams.

My role: research, workflow and system design, AI implementation, review design, launch and ongoing delivery.

02 / AI product experience

Designing how AI
represents a person.

I designed all the prompting behind BlaBlaCar’s live AI-generated review summaries. A few words can change how someone is perceived. The work was to turn product intentions into behavior: what to retain, how to weigh criticism, and how to remain useful, natural and fair.

Define what matters

Distinguish meaningful patterns from incidental remarks, and keep small caveats proportionate to the rest of a person’s feedback.

Judge the reading experience

I combined automated evaluation with blind reading and comparisons. Scores helped diagnose problems; careful reading exposed choices a score could miss.

Bring the behavior into the product

I worked with the product and engineering team to test alternatives and bring the chosen prompting into production.

My role: prompt design, behavioral rules, evaluation and qualitative judgment within the wider product team.

03 / AI adoption across teams

Bringing AI into
everyday work.

I’m BlaBlaCar’s AI Champion for Product & Growth and run the company’s Claude rollout. My work connects tool evaluation, access, onboarding, training and evidence of value, with much of the rollout serving business and non-engineering teams.

Own the rollout

I proposed the Claude rollout, wrote the recommendation and designed its operating process: how colleagues get access, find guidance and document what the tool helps them accomplish. I remain responsible for making it work beyond the initial decision.

Build shared methods

I built a second-brain system and tested its setup process, then developed reusable materials and training with colleagues. The method connects notes, decisions and projects so useful context can build up around a person’s work.

Teach, support and adapt

I co-created and co-delivered second-brain training for Product and colleagues across the company, supported by documentation and office hours. Colleagues have reported setting up and adapting their own systems; ongoing support helps them work through the practical difficulties.

My role: AI Champion for Product & Growth, Claude rollout owner, system builder and co-creator of training and reusable materials.

01 / Exploring

Le Mulot
Paris

A personal map of the city you walk.

Walked streets gradually fill with color. Open the map to see familiar routes, places you’ve discovered, and parts of Paris still waiting for a visit.

View on the App Store ↗

Support · Privacy

A few decisions behind the app +

Keep walking effortless

The core experience records walks in the background. No session to start, no route to plan. I brought the project back to that simple loop when a more elaborate illustrated world made it slow and complicated.

Give the map room

The map keeps its familiar geography. The interface adds a choice of angular Origami or softer Jardin, with shared colors and the same walking history.

Make progress personal

A journal day opens onto the streets recorded that day. The city becomes a way to revisit experience, as well as discover somewhere new.

Two visual languages. One personal map.

02 / Reading

Le Mulot
Lecture

A place for the books that stay with you.

Keep your library, reading dates, ratings and notes together. See your reading take shape through shelves, favorite books, authors and genres.

View on the App Store ↗

Support · Privacy

Inside the next update +

Start with how people read

Add a book when you start it and mark it finished later. Or remember an older read by year. I removed reading-session controls from the everyday flow while keeping the existing history intact.

Make the useful things visible

Reading totals, a visual bookshelf and favorite covers come before the deeper breakdowns. Unknown dates stay unknown, so an old read can belong in a year without being assigned an invented month.

Give editing a clear order

Title, authors, genres and personal reading details come first. One grouped editor keeps secondary information available, with clear Save and Cancel actions.

Make saving trustworthy

Editing a book can update both its details and its reading history. If only part of that save succeeds, the app says so. Cancel leaves the book unchanged, and uncertain saves are not blindly repeated.

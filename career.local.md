## What I optimize for (environment)

The core preference, stated plainly: **I want to be the least-expert person in a room I respect, working on topics I care about.** That environment pulls me up and accelerates me. It is an *environment* preference, not a mission preference.

- I do **not** want to be the teacher, the floor-raiser, the one bringing everyone else up to speed. That drains me rather than feeds me.
- Being surrounded by people more fluent/expert than me, on things I like, is the thing that drives fast growth.
- Reference point: the Silicon Valley period. The value wasn't the place — it was being surrounded by smart people on problems I cared about, evolving faster because of it.

## Screen for a next move

Not "does this company believe in AI" — too vague, everyone says yes now, and it can land me as the most fluent person in the room again (the thing that drains me).

The sharper filter: **Will I be surrounded by people more fluent than me on things I care about, with permission to work at that level?**

This rules out places that would pass the loose "believes in AI" test but where I'd end up ahead of everyone again.

Self-assessed **proficient rather than mastering** this cycle. Two reasons, named plainly:

## External signal — Phil Chen on career advice in the age of AI (2026-07-02)

Phil Chen (founder, ex-OpenAI/DeepMind/Scale) argues AI automates anything gradeable, so the durable work is problem selection and resource allocation, not execution. Points that connect to the filter above:

- **Time, relationships, reputation are the scarce resources** — not capital, which is now cheap. Matches the "least-expert person in a room I respect" logic: the room is the reputation/relationship engine.
- **Problem-finding over problem-solving** — the differentiator is identifying which problems are worth an agent's tokens, not writing the code. Relevant to the "outgrown the room" signal above: if Simon is the one framing problems for others rather than being pushed by sharper framings, that is the same drain from a different angle.
- **Work on the most ambitious form of the problem** — screen a company/role by whether it is tackling the hardest version of its problem and has a real shot, not by whether it "believes in AI."
- **Efficiency in front of goal** — reputation opens the door, but the choice among open doors still has to fit interest and culture, not just theoretical upside (his own Anthropic/Cursor/DeepMind/OpenAI decisions as example).
